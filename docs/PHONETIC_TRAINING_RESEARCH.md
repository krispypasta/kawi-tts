# Phonetic Training Research: Adult L2 Acquisition of Kawi-TTS Phonemes

## 1. Retroflex Consonants (ṭ, ḍ)
*   **Articulatory Cues over Perceptual:** Explicit instruction on physical tongue positioning (tongue tip elevation, dorsum lowering creating a concave shape) is significantly more effective than purely listening to audio targets. 
*   **Oral Motor Exercises (OME):** Non-speech OMEs (isolating jaw, lip, and tongue muscle movements) followed by speech-OMEs (monosyllables) effectively reduce retroflex production errors.
*   **Visual Biofeedback:** Ultrasound tongue imaging is heavily cited as a breakthrough for adults learning retroflexes. If unavailable, physical vocal-tract models or detailed sagittal diagrams are the closest substitute.
*   **Syllabic Asymmetry:** Evidence suggests retroflexes in the coda position are often acquired faster than in the onset position depending on the speaker's L1.

## 2. Consonant Clusters & Epenthesis Suppression
*   **Epenthesis as Default L2 Strategy:** Adult learners natively default to vowel epenthesis (inserting a schwa-like vowel) to break up unfamiliar consonant clusters.
*   **Speech Motor Learning:** Training requires explicit instruction to "attend to the cluster and try not to produce a vowel."
*   **Acoustic Shrinking:** Progress is measured acoustically; successful learners don't drop the epenthetic vowel instantly. Instead, the inserted vowel gets progressively shorter (duration) across sessions before disappearing. Spectrograms can validate this (absence of a periodic vocalic portion with >2 cycles).

## 3. Feedback Loops & Self-Recording
*   **The Perception Bottleneck:** The main barrier is that learners mishear their own live output due to bone conduction. Self-recording forces the brain to hear objective reality.
*   **PME Cycle (Planning, Monitoring, Evaluation):** 
    1. Pre-identify target phoneme hurdles.
    2. Record minimum three takes (requires a "cold listen-back").
    3. Self-evaluate against a native/target baseline and log specific errors (e.g., "inserted schwa between /k/ and /r/").
*   **Diagnostic Audits:** Use "minimal pair" recordings to force the brain to stop mapping foreign sounds to the closest L1 equivalent (combating "categorical perception").

## Validation Methods for the Project
1. **Acoustic Spectrograms:** Validate cluster epenthesis by checking for F2/F3 formant structures between consonants.
2. **Minimal Pair Baseline Testing:** Record and compare ṭ/t and ḍ/d contrasts blindly.
3. **Spreadsheet Tracking:** Log the duration of intrusive schwas in clusters over multiple weeks to track motor memory acquisition.
